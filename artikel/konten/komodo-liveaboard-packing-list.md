# The Ultimate Komodo Liveaboard Packing List

Not sure what to pack for your Komodo liveaboard? This complete guide covers everything
you need to bring.

Before we get to the items, we need to talk about the bag itself.

Suitcases vs. Soft Bags: What to Bring on Board

You can bring a hard-shell suitcase on board, but for maximum comfort during your
liveaboard trip, we highly recommend packing your gear in a medium-sized suitcase or a
more flexible, soft-sided bag like a duffel or a travel backpack.

While Phinisi boats offer spacious cabins, traditional maritime design means storage space
is often tucked under beds or in uniquely shaped compartments. Opting for a flexible bag
makes storing your luggage effortless and gives you much more room to move around your
cabin.

If you are traveling through Indonesia with a massive hard-shell suitcase, you are still
welcome to bring it on board, just keep in mind that it will limit your available cabin space.
Alternatively, most tour operators in Labuan Bajo will happily let you leave your bulky main
luggage at their office, allowing you to take a smaller, more practical bag onto the boat.


1. Apparel: What to Wear on the Water
You will spend 90% of your time in swimwear or lightweight lounging clothes, but the
micro-climates of Komodo National Park can surprise you.

   ● Swimwear (3-4 sets): Bring multiple sets. The humidity means clothes take a long
      time to dry on deck, and you do not want to put on a damp swimsuit for your
      afternoon snorkel.
   ● Lightweight, Long-Sleeve Rash Guard: The sun near the equator is unforgiving. A
      UV-protective rash guard is essential for snorkeling—it protects your back from
      sunburns much better than sunscreen alone.
   ● Windbreaker or Light Fleece Jacket: This is the most forgotten item! Evenings on
      the upper deck of a moving ship can get surprisingly chilly, especially when the trade
      winds blow between July and August.
   ● Breathable Evening Wear: Linen shirts, light cotton dresses, or relaxed shorts for
      dinner under the stars.


2. Footwear: The Reality of the Hikes
Many generic lists tell you to bring heavy hiking boots for Komodo. Do not bring them. They
are bulky and completely unnecessary.

   ● A Good Pair of Running Shoes: The famous hike to the viewpoint on Padar Island
      is steep, but it is entirely paved with a wooden and stone staircase. A comfortable
      pair of running shoes with decent cushioning and grip is all you need.

   ● Slip-On Sandals or Slides: You will be barefoot while walking around the wooden
      decks of the Phinisi. You only need sandals for quick island hops to Pink Beach or
      Kelor Island.
   ● Water Shoes (Optional but Recommended): Some snorkeling spots have shallow,
      sharp coral. Water shoes make getting in and out of the water much safer.


3. Toiletries & Health Essentials
Komodo is a protected marine reserve, and the ecosystem is fragile. What you bring on your
skin eventually ends up in the water.

   ● Reef-Safe Sunscreen: This is non-negotiable. Standard sunscreens contain
      oxybenzone and octinoxate, which bleach and kill coral reefs. Look for mineral-based
      sunscreens containing zinc oxide.
   ● Motion Sickness Medication: Even if you don't usually get seasick, the ocean can
      be unpredictable. Bring Dramamine or Antimo (the local Indonesian brand). Pro-tip:
      Take it 30 minutes before the boat sets sail, not after you start feeling sick.
   ● Leave-In Conditioner / Detangler: The combination of salty water and constant sea
      wind will turn long hair into a bird's nest. A good leave-in conditioner is a lifesaver.
   ● Basic First Aid & Meds: While all Kapalsantai partner boats carry first-aid kits, bring
      your own ibuprofen, band-aids, and any personal prescriptions.


4. Gadget & Photography Gear
You are going to one of the most photogenic places on earth, but you will be completely off
the grid for days at a time.

   ● High-Capacity Power Bank: While boats have charging outlets in the cabins, the
      generator may be turned off during certain hours of the night to ensure a quiet sleep.
   ● Extra SD Cards / SSD Storage: You will shoot hundreds of high-resolution photos
      and 4K videos. Because there is virtually zero cellular signal out at sea, you cannot
      rely on cloud backups. Bring physical storage.
   ● Waterproof Phone Pouch or GoPro: You will want to capture the Manta Rays and
      sea turtles. If using a phone pouch, test it in the sink at home before trusting it in the
      ocean.
   ● Drone (With Caveats): Drones capture incredible footage of the islands, but you
      must have a specific permit to fly within Komodo National Park. Ask your Kapalsantai
      agent about arranging this before you arrive.


What NOT to Bring
   ● Single-Use Plastics: Your boat will provide drinking water, so bring a high-quality
      insulated reusable bottle (like a Hydro Flask or Yeti) to keep your water ice-cold on
      the deck.

   ● Heavy Denim or Jeans: In the tropical humidity, denim will stick to your legs, restrict
      your movement, and take three days to dry if it gets wet. Leave the jeans in your
      hotel.
